My Projects

I enjoy repairing and improving well-designed products that deserve a longer life than they sometimes get due to design flaws, worn components, or age. Over the years, I have repaired, restored, and upgraded a wide variety of devices and everyday objects, often extending their usable lifespan far beyond what was originally expected.

My motivation is not primarily driven by sustainability or ideology. Instead, it comes from genuine curiosity and a passion for understanding how things work. I enjoy taking broken devices apart, exploring their internal design, identifying the root cause of a problem, and finding a practical solution. Every repair is an opportunity to learn something new, improve an existing design, and bring a well-built product back into everyday use.

As the saying goes, "One person's trash is another person's treasure." I have found that many items people consider beyond saving simply need the right diagnosis, a bit of patience, and someone willing to give them a second chance.

My own child: Akku

Since the official HyperX software only displayed the headset battery status as Low, Medium, and High, Windows was also unable to display the battery status at all. Because I preferred not to rely on additional third-party applications, I decided to reverse engineer the feature and implement it in my own Python application. The same application also allows me to switch Windows audio devices much faster than using the standard Windows sound settings.

To achieve this, I monitored the headset's USB communication and analyzed the exchanged data until I identified which values represented the battery level. After successfully decoding the protocol, I determined the values corresponding to 0% and 100% battery, as well as the intermediate levels by correlating them with the headset's battery warning sounds.

The application now writes the current battery percentage to a battery.txt file, which is read by Rainmeter and displayed on the desktop in real time.

I also created a custom Rainmeter widget that displays real-time pollen information by retrieving data directly from the University of Turku's Norkko API.

The application remains an active personal project, and I continue to refine and expand its functionality. As it is still evolving, I have chosen not to distribute it publicly at this time.

SoundEffect Program

SoundEffect is another personal project that is still under development, although I have already built a working prototype. The project taught me a great deal about the challenges of capturing and processing audio in real time, especially when aiming for low-latency sound effects.

The application runs a WebSocket server that communicates with an HTML-based user interface. A separate component of the application listens to the audio stream, processes it in real time, and sends the required data through the WebSocket connection, allowing the HTML interface to render the visual effects instantly.

Any application capable of displaying the HTML interface can use these visual effects. In my case, I use them as a Browser Source in OBS Studio, allowing the effects to appear in real time during livestreams and recordings.

Development is currently on hold, but I may continue expanding the project in the future if inspiration strikes again. Since the project is still subject to change, I have chosen not to release the source code publicly at this time.

Bluetooth Speaker Rehousing

The original enclosure had already been damaged during shipping and was no longer airtight, which significantly reduced the speaker's bass performance. To solve this problem, I designed and built a completely new acrylic enclosure while also adding several practical improvements and custom features.

The project involved millimeter-precise planning, fabrication, and continuous refinement to achieve a clean and durable result. The speaker is powered by an integrated power bank, allowing it to recharge itself internally and providing several days of battery life on a single charge.
